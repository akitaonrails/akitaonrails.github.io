From what I could research, on December 27, 2006, Discovery Channel aired the documentary "The iPod Revolution: Inside Story of the Apple iPod and Steve Jobs."

This documentary is very well produced. In 40 minutes it sums up Apple's history from 1997 to 2006, covering almost a decade that runs from Steve Jobs' return to the launch of the iPod and the huge success of the iTunes Music Store. And that's only half the story: the iPhone wouldn't be revealed until January 2007, so the plot is even bigger.

I always recommend researching this story because it's very interesting and engaging. For more details, read the books:

- [The Perfect Thing: How the iPod Shuffles Commerce, Culture, and Coolness](http://www.amazon.com/Perfect-Thing-Shuffles-Commerce-Coolness/dp/B001GVJCF6/ref=pd_sim_b_46) – by Steven Levy.
- [The Cult of the iPod](http://www.amazon.com/Cult-iPod-Leander-Kahney/dp/1593270666/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&s=books&qid=1296395947&sr=8-1) – by Leander Kahney, who also appears in the documentary.
- [iCon Steve Jobs: The Greatest Second Act in the History of Business](http://www.amazon.com/iCon-Steve-Jobs-Greatest-Business/dp/0471787841/ref=sr_1_1?s=books&ie=UTF8&qid=1296396002&sr=1-1) – by Jeffrey Young and William Simon.

But the story is much more interesting than that. I've read and watched all the available material on the subject since 1983. Searching around, I found some pieces of this story on YouTube too. Here are more video links:

- [1983](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSiQA6KKyJo) – In this keynote, a young Steve Jobs, who had just turned Apple into a superpower, talks about his plans for a new computer to launch in 1984. It's an epic speech. At the end he unveils the most famous commercial in the world, [1984](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OYecfV3ubP8), directed by Ridley Scott and shown only once, during the following year's Super Bowl.

- [Boston Macworld 1997](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEHNrqPkefI) – Steve Jobs was pushed out of Apple in 1985 and returns more than 11 years later to a devastated Apple, losing $1 billion a year, with people like Michael Dell saying [If I were Apple, I'd shut it down and give the money back to the shareholders](http://www.nytimes.com/2006/01/16/technology/16apple.html). He lays out the problems, points to Apple's still-strong assets, and sketches a recovery strategy that runs flat against every "analyst." I recommend it to anyone who cares about management: how to get back up after a fall.

- [Macworld 1998](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0BHPtoTctDY) – A year after his return, Steve had already reshaped all of Apple and launches the first product that marks the reboot: the colorful iMac. It was a machine aimed mainly at the nascent consumer internet market. Bear in mind, it's still the early days of the first internet bubble.

- [iPod Introduction 2001](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vy01RzqA5RA) – Clip from the presentation where Steve shows the iPod for the first time. It was a closed event inside Apple's campus, for invited journalists only. The first day of a revolution none of those present could have imagined.

- [iTunes Store Introduction 2003](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B2n86TROxzY) – With software like Napster and Limewire letting people download music for free, how do you sell a system where you have to pay for music? This video shows how Steve pulled it off. The most important part was bending the Big 5 of the music industry into accepting a single flat price for individual tracks and albums, and getting artists to break albums apart and sell individual songs.

- "iPhone Introduction 2007" – This one is genius. After two and a half years working in secret, amid all the growing rumors about an iPod-phone, watch how Steve redefined the smartphone market. One fundamental point: control of the handset had always belonged to the carriers, but Steve negotiated an unprecedented contract with Cingular (today AT&T) where the phone stayed under Apple's control. That meant no pre-installed marketing apps and nothing of the sort. This year the exclusivity contract with AT&T ended, and Apple just announced the CDMA iPhone for Verizon's potential 93 million customers.

To close: in the documentary, Apple starts 1996 in terrible shape, losing $1 billion a year, with shares falling to $17 each. Now, at the close of [the last quarter of 2010](http://business.financialpost.com/2011/01/18/apple-reports-record-breaking-quarterly-results-again/), it sold 16.24 million iPhones in that quarter alone, plus 7.33 million iPads, 19.45 million iPods, and 4.13 million Macs. Again: in the quarter alone.

So far Apple has sold more than 14 million iPads since the launch, back in April of last year. And the share price is now $340. Whoever bought in 1996 and held saw their capital grow almost 80 times in 15 years, counting the splits Apple did in 2000 and 2005 (via @cassiozen's comment below). An excellent investment.
